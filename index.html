The problem

The cabin is on the lake.
But most buyers never really see it.

The biggest financial risk in a lake purchase isn't the deck or the septic — it's the water you can't evaluate from your phone.

You find out the lake after the offer

Listings show square feet and shoreline footage. They don't show that the "crystal-clear" lake turns pea-soup green in August — or that Secchi readings have been trending the wrong way for years.

Invasive species change everything

Zebra mussels cut swimmers' feet and wreck boat motors. Eurasian milfoil chokes shorelines. None of this appears in listing photos — but it's all in DNR records, if you know where to look.

“Is the fishing good?” is guesswork

Fish surveys, special regulations, consumption advisories — they exist for nearly every Minnesota and Wisconsin lake. But hunting across five state websites after a 10-hour showing day? Nobody does that.

You don’t even know which lake it is

A street address doesn't say which lake it's “on.” Is it connected to the big one nearby? Is there a public access? CabinClarity answers both before the first showing.

Diligence eats into your evenings

What CabinClarity does in seconds is currently hours of tab-juggling per lake — DNR LakeFinder, LakeBrowser, county GIS, flood maps. The data exists. It's just scattered. We centralize it.

“Lake access” isn’t lakefront

Listings love the phrase “lake access” — but a shared dock two streets away is a very different property (and price) than one with your own shoreline. CabinClarity shows exactly which lake a home sits on, and how it touches the water.
